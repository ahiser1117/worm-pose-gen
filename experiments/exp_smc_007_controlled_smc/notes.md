# EXP-SMC-007 — controlled known-pose SMC recovery

## Frozen scope

This is an algorithm-recovery experiment on synthetic latent random walks. It
uses the three real strict EXP-SMC-002B anchors only as initial shape and width
profiles. Every subsequent state is generated by an explicitly synthetic,
zero-drift, block-diagonal random walk required by the immutable EXP-SMC-006
decision addendum. No empirical or natural-motion transition claim is allowed.

Calibration uses nominal renderer-matched sequences and seed 7001. Evaluation
uses held-out seeds 7101/7102. The particle grid is 32/64/128/256/512 and the
soft-Dice temperature grid is 0.01/0.03. Rendering is at 183×242. The protected
2025 holdout remains closed.

## Comparisons and diagnostics

Compare hold, exact two-anchor latent interpolation, bootstrap SMC MAP
genealogy, and the same particle genealogy terminally reweighted by the known
right anchor. Record ESS, resampling, root diversity, truth survival, trajectory
and endpoint error, runtime, seeds, and full trajectories.

Evaluate nominal, 0.5×/2× truth-process scale, middle-frame observation
dropout, 1.15× observation-width mismatch, and partial FOV. A controlled
self-contact bridge is omitted prospectively because forcing a coil would no
longer be a zero-drift random walk and would invent the dynamics this
experiment is forbidden to claim.

## Frozen gate

On held-out nominal sequences require median forward SMC trajectory error ≤8
px, terminal-reweighted trajectory error ≤6 px, and truth survival in at least
75% of frames. Across each stress condition require terminal-reweighted median
trajectory error ≤12 px. Passing means controlled synthetic algorithm recovery
only, never natural difficult-bout success.

## Status

`SUPPORTED_CONTROLLED_SYNTHETIC_ONLY`

The independently calibrated setting was 128 particles with soft-Dice
temperature 0.03. On the two held-out nominal seeds, median trajectory errors
were 2.19 px for forward bootstrap SMC and 1.95 px for terminal right-anchor
reweighting; truth survival was 1.00 and median ESS was 0.89 of the particle
count. Terminal-reweighted median trajectory error remained below the frozen
12 px stress gate for 0.5× process scale (2.23 px), 2× process scale (4.38
px), middle-observation dropout (2.15 px), 1.15× width mismatch (2.95 px),
and partial FOV (2.86 px). All frozen checks passed.

The exact two-anchor interpolation comparator uses the known synthetic final
anchor, so its zero final-frame error is by construction; it is a controlled
oracle comparator, not a causal tracker. Nevertheless, its median trajectory
error was lowest in all six scenarios. Terminal right-anchor reweighting
improved forward SMC only for nominal and middle-observation-dropout sequences
(2/6 scenarios) and worsened it for the other four. The post-result scientific
decision is therefore: controlled algorithm execution and truth survival are
supported on these synthetic sequences; a general terminal-anchor smoothing
benefit, SMC superiority over interpolation, and H8 are **not supported**.

These results establish recovery on renderer-matched synthetic random walks
only. They do not validate a natural motion prior or authorize natural
difficult-bout SMC. Full metrics are in `metrics.json`; large trajectories are
stored at the configured external path with the SHA-256 recorded in that file.

The bounded implementation ran on CPU and resampled after every weighted
nonterminal step (three events), while recording ESS diagnostically; it did not
test an adaptive ESS trigger or establish GPU throughput. Those are algorithmic
limitations of this smoke test, not properties of the final intended tracker.
